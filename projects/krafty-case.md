Krafty Case sells laser-engraved wood iPhone cases: a cherry or maple face over a shock-absorbing TPU frame, engraved with Hawaiʻi motifs like honu and hibiscus alongside art-deco patterns. I started it to find out whether I could run an entire business rather than just build the website for one, so I did all of it. I registered the LLC, obtained an EIN from the IRS, opened business banking, sourced the product, and built the storefront on Shopify. I shot the product photography myself on Oʻahu, wrote the brand and product copy, ran paid acquisition through Meta Ads, and fulfilled orders from Hawaiʻi to the mainland using Shopify-generated shipping labels through USPS.

My role was every role. There was nobody to hand a problem to, which meant the parts of a business I would never have chosen to learn became the parts I spent the most time on: sales tax, refund policy, shipping thresholds, customer service response times, and what a customer actually expects when a package is late. The store took real orders and produced real revenue, and I closed out every order without a refund dispute. Running the whole stack also changed how I read other businesses. When I now research a client before building their site, I am reading it as someone who has had to price a product, pay for an ad that did not convert, and answer the email afterward.

Krafty Case is on hiatus, and the honest reason is that I underestimated customer acquisition. Inventory turned out to be manageable once Shopify was tracking it, but ad spend was not. Paid acquisition consistently ate the margin on a product priced in the twenties, and I had not modeled that before committing to inventory. What I got in exchange was a real education in targeting a domestic audience and reading campaign performance rather than guessing at it. The business also ran through a stretch when my father had a stroke and my grandfather passed away while I was in school, and running every function of a company alone through that was not sustainable. I closed it out with sales on the books and no unresolved orders, which I am satisfied with. The next time I take on a product business, I will calculate acquisition cost against unit margin before I order inventory rather than after, and I will not attempt to be the only person in the company.
